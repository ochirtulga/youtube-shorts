# The Viking King Who Bargained With The Pope: CapCut Stickman Prompts

Long-form, horizontal (16:9) stickman explainer, about 11 minutes of narration (about 1,700 words at 150 to 160 words per minute), plus a 20-second end screen, split into 8 chapters.

## How to use this file

1. CapCut video design may cap prompt length or video length per generation, so generate **one chapter at a time**.
2. For each chapter, paste the **MASTER STYLE BLOCK** first, then that chapter's block, into one prompt.
3. Keep every generated chapter, then put them on one timeline in order (Chapter 1 to 8) and export as one video.
4. If a chapter is still too long for CapCut, split it at the line marked `[SPLIT POINT]` and generate the two halves separately, pasting the master block before each half.
5. If CapCut accepts very long prompts and long videos, you can paste the master block followed by all 8 chapter blocks in a single prompt.

```

Limited accent colors: red for Vikings, war and danger; gold for kings, the Church and money; blue for diplomacy, deals and peace; grey for ordinary people. Settings and objects in simple line art with flat color fills. Smooth, bouncy character animation: walking, pointing, shrugging, gasping, arguing, bowing, shaking hands. VISUAL DEVICES: Animated parchment maps with dotted travel lines and kingdoms filling with color. Bold labels, names and dates that pop in (for example "1027", "ENGLAND", "UNJUST TOLLS"). Arrows, money bags and flowing silver coins to show money. Simple icons, thought bubbles and split screens for comparisons. Every analogy in the narration is shown literally and humorously.

PACING: A new visual, gag or camera move every 3 to 6 seconds. Never hold a static frame longer than 6 seconds. Smooth zooms, pans and quick whip transitions. VOICE: One narrator for the entire video. Warm, energetic, confident male storyteller, conversational, like a favorite history teacher telling an incredible story to friends. About 150 to 160 words per minute. Lively pitch variety, strong emphasis on key words, names and numbers, short dramatic pauses before reveals, a smile in the voice on the funny analogies. Never monotone, never sleepy, never robotic. Read the narration EXACTLY as written, word for word, with nothing skipped, summarized or added. Pronunciation: Cnut = "kuh-NOOT", Sweyn = "SWAIN", Conrad = "KON-rad", pallium = "PAL-ee-um", Christendom = "KRISS-en-dum", Burgundy = "BUR-gun-dee".

CAPTIONS: Clean subtitles at the bottom center, white text with a thin black outline, maximum 2 lines, perfectly synced to the voice. Names and numbers in gold.

MUSIC: Light cinematic background music, always quiet under the voice. Nordic drums and low strings for Viking and war moments, warm choir and soft organ for Rome and the Church, playful plucked strings for the funny analogies, a gentle rising theme for victories.

SOUND EFFECTS: Soft whooshes on transitions, pops when labels appear, coin clinks for money, sword clangs for battles, crowd murmur in Rome, quill scratching for letters. Never louder than the narration.
```

## Accuracy notes and suggested script fixes

- **"Cnut the Great died a decade later":** Cnut died in November 1035, about eight years after the 1027 journey. Suggested fix: "Cnut the Great died eight years later, ruler of a vast empire."
- **"He also ruled Denmark, Norway, and parts of Sweden" (Chapter 4, before Rome):** Cnut ruled Denmark by 1019, but he only took Norway in 1028, after the Rome trip. In his 1027 letter he styles himself king of the Norwegians and of part of the Swedes, which was a claim more than control. Suggested fix: "He also ruled Denmark, claimed Norway and part of Sweden, and would soon conquer Norway outright."
- **"a kingdom weary of war finally bent the knee":** after the Battle of Assandun (1016), Cnut and Edmund Ironside first split England between them. Edmund died weeks later, and Cnut became king of all England. The line is a fair simplification.
- **The 1027 letter** survives through later chroniclers. In it Cnut says Conrad II and King Rudolf of Burgundy agreed his people could travel without unjust tolls, and that the Pope agreed the archbishops' pallium payments were excessive. Historians debate how fully these promises were kept.
- **"Horned helmets are a myth"** is correct, which is why every Viking in the prompts wears a round helmet.
